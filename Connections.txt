=====================================================================
 ESP32 3D ROOM SCANNER - WIRING / CONNECTIONS (v2, with power details)
 ESP32 + 28BYJ-48 stepper (pan) + servo (tilt) + Acconeer XM125 radar
=====================================================================

BOARD:  ESP32 Dev Module

---------------------------------------------------------------------
1. SIGNAL PIN MAP (as used in radar_scanner.ino)
---------------------------------------------------------------------

Component                Component pin     ESP32 pin    Notes
-----------------------  ----------------  -----------  ------------------------------
Stepper driver ULN2003   IN1               GPIO 19      28BYJ-48 stepper, horizontal pan
                         IN2               GPIO 18
                         IN3               GPIO 5
                         IN4               GPIO 17

Servo (vertical tilt)    Signal            GPIO 16      Uses ESP32Servo library

Acconeer XM125 radar     TX  (radar out)   GPIO 25      ESP32 Serial2 RX
(UART)                   RX  (radar in)    GPIO 26      ESP32 Serial2 TX
                         WAKE_UP           3V3          Tied permanently to the ESP32 3V3 pin
                         Baud rate         921600       8N1

Start switch / button    One side          GPIO 14      INPUT_PULLUP; grounding it starts a scan
                         Other side        GND

PC / USB serial          USB cable         -            115200 baud (e.g. COM10 on Windows)

Note on the radar UART: names in the sketch are from the ESP32's point
of view. Radar TX goes to ESP32 GPIO 25, radar RX goes to ESP32 GPIO 26
(TX <-> RX crossed).

---------------------------------------------------------------------
2. POWER WIRING (as built)
---------------------------------------------------------------------

  Power source : the ESP32 board's 5 V supply (5V / VIN pin; when the
                 board is powered over USB this is the USB 5 V).
  Distribution : breadboard power rails. The 5 V rail is fed from the
                 ESP32 5 V pin, and the GND rail is joined to ESP32 GND.

  Connected to the 5 V rail:
    - ULN2003 driver board (+) -> powers the 28BYJ-48 stepper
    - Servo power (red wire)
    - Acconeer XM125 supply pin

  Connected to the ESP32 3V3 pin:
    - Acconeer XM125 WAKE_UP pin (held high so the sensor stays awake)

  Connected to the GND rail (common ground):
    - ESP32 GND, ULN2003 GND, servo GND (brown/black), XM125 GND,
      start switch (other side)

  Everything shares one ground.

---------------------------------------------------------------------
3. STEPPER (28BYJ-48 + ULN2003 board)
---------------------------------------------------------------------

  ESP32 GPIO 19  ->  ULN2003 IN1
  ESP32 GPIO 18  ->  ULN2003 IN2
  ESP32 GPIO 5   ->  ULN2003 IN3
  ESP32 GPIO 17  ->  ULN2003 IN4
  ULN2003 +/-    ->  5 V rail and GND rail
  28BYJ-48       ->  plugs into the ULN2003 5-pin connector

  Software order for the Stepper library: IN1, IN3, IN2, IN4
      Stepper myStepper(2048, IN1, IN3, IN2, IN4);
  Steps per revolution: 2048   (90 degrees = 512 steps)

---------------------------------------------------------------------
4. SERVO
---------------------------------------------------------------------

  Servo signal (orange/yellow)  ->  ESP32 GPIO 16
  Servo power (red)             ->  5 V rail
  Servo ground (brown/black)    ->  GND rail

---------------------------------------------------------------------
5. RADAR (Acconeer XM125)
---------------------------------------------------------------------

  Radar TX      ->  ESP32 GPIO 25  (Serial2 RX)
  Radar RX      ->  ESP32 GPIO 26  (Serial2 TX)
  Radar WAKE_UP ->  ESP32 3V3
  Radar supply  ->  5 V rail
  Radar GND     ->  GND rail

  Output text format, one line per measurement:
      1 detected distances: 2.471099 m
  The firmware reads up to the newline, finds the ':' and converts the
  number from metres to millimetres.

  Check the XM125 datasheet for the allowed supply voltage on your
  module and for its other pins (interface select, reset), and
  confirm they are wired for UART mode.

---------------------------------------------------------------------
6. POWER NOTES / RISKS
---------------------------------------------------------------------

  All loads run from the ESP32's 5 V pin, so the total current is
  limited by whatever feeds the board (usually the USB port, often
  about 500 mA). Typical draw is roughly:
      28BYJ-48 stepper  ~ 200-250 mA while moving
      Servo             ~ 100-500+ mA, with spikes when it moves
      ESP32             ~ 80-250 mA (more with WiFi)
      XM125 radar       see datasheet
  Possible symptoms of an overloaded supply: ESP32 resets or
  brown-outs, servo jitter, noisy or missing radar readings.

  Ways to reduce the risk:
    - Use a good USB power source, or feed 5 V from a separate supply
      (2 A or more) into the rail, with its GND joined to the ESP32 GND.
    - Add a 470 uF - 1000 uF capacitor across the servo 5 V and GND.
    - Keep the stepper and servo off the radar's supply line if the
      readings are noisy.
    - Do not feed an external 5 V supply and USB into the ESP32 at the
      same time unless the board has protection against it.

---------------------------------------------------------------------
7. SCAN BEHAVIOUR (summary)
---------------------------------------------------------------------

  - Start: stepper at 0 deg (centre), servo at 90 deg.
  - Ground GPIO 14 (or send the character 'S' over USB serial) to scan.
  - Stepper moves to -90 deg (-512 steps), then sweeps -90..+90 deg and
    back, alternating direction each row.
  - Servo steps from 90 deg to 170 deg in 5 deg increments, one row each.
  - The servo is detached while the stepper sweeps, to avoid PWM jitter.
  - At the end the servo returns to 90 deg and the stepper to centre.

  The stepper has no home sensor: the position at power-up or reset is
  treated as 0 deg. Point the radar straight ahead before powering on.

---------------------------------------------------------------------
8. SERIAL OUTPUT TO THE PC (115200 baud)
---------------------------------------------------------------------

  Data line:    <stepper_step>,<servo_angle>,<distance_mm>
  Example:      -512,90,2471.1
  Status lines start with '#' (for example #SCAN_START, #SCAN_END)
  and should be ignored by the Python parser.

  Close the Arduino Serial Monitor before running the Python viewer;
  only one program can use the COM port at a time.

---------------------------------------------------------------------
10. KNOWN NOTES
---------------------------------------------------------------------

  - GPIO 5 is a boot strapping pin; it works for the ULN2003 input but
    avoid pulling it low during reset.
  - The XM125 beam is wide, so edges in the scan are soft.
  - The 28BYJ-48 has gear backlash; rows scanned in opposite directions
    can be shifted slightly (see BACKLASH_STEPS in the Python file).
